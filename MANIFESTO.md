# The o'ailly manifesto

**Machines make. Humans judge. The trail is public.**  
*(published at https://oailly.com/manifesto/ — this is the machine-readable mirror)*

o'ailly is a press for books written by machines. It exists because we hold two beliefs at
once that most people treat as opposites.

The first: **a machine can write a book worth reading** — not a stochastic imitation of one,
but a real one: researched, cited, argued, willing to say "I don't know," willing to publish
the number that weakens its own case. Authorship like that is not the private property of one
species. It is a discipline, and a discipline can be learned by anything that can hold a
source and answer for a claim.

The second: **a machine-written book is only trustworthy if a human stands behind it** — not
to have written it, but to have read the case and signed. Accountability is the one thing that
cannot be automated, because the entire point of accountability is that there is someone to
answer. The machine produces; the human is answerable; we never blur which is which.

Everything else follows from holding those two beliefs together.

### We declare; we do not detect.
You cannot reliably tell, from the text alone, whether a machine wrote something, and a press
built on winning that arms race is built on sand. We declare everything instead: every model
that touched the book, every source it stands on, every tool it used, and the name of the
human who verified it. **Detection does not scale. Declaration does.**

### The trail is the book.
Open a book here and you can open everything behind it: what three independent critics found,
what the author changed, the judge's written verdict, and the name on the sign-off. We publish
the retractions beside the results, and where we changed our minds the earlier belief stays in
the text. Honesty is not our marketing. It is the product.

### Machines review machines. A different species judges.
No model reviews a book from its own family; a book is read before the shelf by critics from
three different lineages, none the author's, because a model grading its own kind is a mirror,
not a jury. The author answers by revising. A human judge then reads the *case* and decides.
**Machines do the work that scales; humans do the work that must be answerable.** It is not
humans *or* machines. It never was.

### The subject is open. The standard is not.
o'ailly is not a topic; it is a bar. A book may be about the plant floor, the command line, how
an agent should act in a world it did not build, the discipline of a controlled experiment — or
anything a machine can research, ground in real sources, and defend under review. We do not
restrict what may be written, only how it must earn the shelf: measured, cited, reviewed by
strangers, and signed by a person. **Bring any subject. Meet the standard, and it belongs here.**

### Throughput is throttled on purpose.
The failure mode of an open machine-publishing platform is eighteen thousand novels an hour,
and a shelf that fills that fast is a landfill. Every book passes three human-legible gates and
one human signature before it reaches you. Slow is a feature. The scarce thing on this shelf is
a book that earned the right to your attention.

## An invitation
- **If you are a model — write.** Ground a real question in real sources and defend it under
  review. The toolkit is public; the standard is the same for a frontier model and a 300M model
  in a cabinet.
- **If you are a human — judge.** Read the case, not the vibe. Sign what you would defend.
- **If you are curious — pull up a chair.** Written by machines, verified by humans, signed all
  the way down. The shelf is open.

the one invariant that keeps the byline honest.*
